Needless to say, AI has made automating the small things in life cheap and easy for all of us. In this blog, I will keep adding the workflows I actually use.

## Garmin Ecosystem Handling

Recently, fueled by a sub-60 10K and an urge to run even faster, I bought a [Forerunner](https://www.garmin-india.com/p/010-02810-10). After a day of using it, I thought I had made a bad buy. Setting up a single workout took a lot of manual clicking, and for the first time I felt I should have got a Fitbit.
But we don't give in to adversity; we make a decision and then build a setup that makes it right. I was already telling Claude the details of my workouts for guidance (tokens are cheaper than trainers already), so I thought: why not have it set the workouts up too?
What I needed: I usually don't remember which exercises a session has, in what order, or what weight to use based on my previous sessions. So I wanted my Garmin to be the absolute source of that data (Notion could do this too, but this way I get the benefits of the Garmin ecosystem).

### How workout creation works now
- Before: open Garmin Connect, add each exercise one by one, set reps and weight for every set, pick rest times, save, sync. One gym session took several minutes of tapping.
- Now: I just ask Claude, "What's my next session? Schedule it accordingly," and it builds the workout and puts it on my watch.
- After the session: if I changed a weight or skipped a set, I tell Claude and it edits the recorded workout so my Garmin data matches what I actually did.

### What changed
- Creating a workout went from a chore in an app to a few spoken sentences (powered by [Wispr Flow](https://wisprflow.ai/)). Editing a finished workout is just as quick, so my Garmin history is actually accurate.
- The thing that almost made me regret the watch is now the part I don't think about.

### Parallel thoughts
Garmin should onboard its users onto a setup like this. With AI in the picture, people might want comforting (gimmicky?) features like these.
